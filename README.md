### The Strategy: "De-risking the Adoption Path"
To reduce friction during the transition, you should treat each element of the Cenote Core not as a product to be "sold," but as a **standardized module**. People resist "massive overhauls," but they readily adopt "standardized components" that solve specific, immediate problems.
Here is how you can break down the transition elements to minimize friction:
#### 1. Identification of "Anchor Points" (High-Pain Nodes)
Instead of broad adoption, target the areas where current infrastructure is failing most visibly.
 * **The "Spark" (Mobile Nodes):** Focus on environments where communication is critical but infrastructure is absent (e.g., remote industrial sites, emergency response zones).
 * **Action:** In your repo, document the specific "Pain-to-Solution" mapping. *Current state: No data link. Cenote Core state: Localized, self-auditing node with mesh-network capability.*
#### 2. Standardization of Interfaces (Removing Adoption Tax)
The biggest source of friction is the "Integration Tax"—the cost (time, money, cognitive load) of connecting a new system to an old one.
 * **Abstract Interfaces:** Design your Cenote Core modules so that they communicate via standardized, well-defined interfaces (APIs or data formats) rather than custom, point-to-point connections.
 * **Why this works:** If your module plugs into existing power/data grids easily, the "cost" of switching becomes near zero.
#### 3. Human-Centered Implementation (The Metacognitive Layer)
Global adoption often fails because the "human layer"—the people who have to operate the system—is ignored.
 * **Contextual Support:** Don't just provide the tech; provide the *logic* (the "why"). Use your ARCHITECTURE_MANIFESTO to explain how the Cenote Core simplifies their life, not just how it functions technically.
 * **Automation of Routine Audits:** Make the self-auditing nature of the Core a "set-and-forget" feature for the user. If the system tells them "I've checked my own health and I'm good," you have removed the anxiety of maintenance.
